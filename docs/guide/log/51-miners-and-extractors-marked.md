# Step 51 · Miners and extractors marked where a line starts

**Goal:** see at a glance where a belt or pipe comes from a node (asked 2026-11-19): "a nice red circle for miners, with a tooltip, and blue for extractors, or better yet, the colour of the fluid or gas they carry". Branch `feat/optimal-arrangement`, on top of step 50, uncommitted.

## What changed

- **On the map**, where a routed belt starts at a miner, its dark dot becomes a **red circle** with a white rim; at a water, oil or resource-well extractor, a circle in the **colour of its fluid** as the game gives it (Water `#7ab0d4`, Crude Oil `#190019`, Nitrogen Gas `#595959`, …). The white rim keeps a dark fluid visible on the dark map. Pointing at it says what it is and what it sends: *Miner Mk.3 and 2 more · Copper Ore · 1074/min*. Stations and drone ports keep their dark dot. **Outside the area too** (asked after): where a routed belt leaves the floor to join the existing belt from a miner or extractor outside the area, the circle stands at that building, at the start of the dotted existing belt, saying what the routed belts joining it take (`wayMark`). Not at the routed belt's end on the floor's edge: the amber ring stands there.
- **In 3D**, a drum on the ground at the foot of the lift, or at the start of the existing belt outside, in the same colour, with the same tooltip; it fades with its belt when factories or levels are picked.
- **Where the colour comes from:** the Docs file gives every item an `mFluidColor`, written `(B=212,G=176,R=122,A=0)`. The catalog now keeps it for fluids and gases as `Item.FluidColour` (`#rrggbb`), and `GET /catalog/items` returns it as `fluidColour`. Solids are white there, so they get none, and miners take a fixed red. Whether a node building is a miner or an extractor comes from the catalog's extractors (`GET /catalog/extractors`, their `form`), not from class names in the web.
- **A catalog import is needed** for the colours: the new column is empty (null) for items already stored. Until then extractors are drawn blue (`#1e88e5`), and miners already show red.

## Also fixed: machines in 3D missing walls

Seen 2026-11-19 on Dark Matter Residue: the machines on the belt floor in 3D looked like open boxes. `boxGeometry` (`area/box-geometry.ts`) listed each side's corners facing outwards in the game's axes, but both 3D views place a corner at (x, height, y): swapping two axes mirrors the box and turns every side inside out. The area view hid it with `THREE.DoubleSide`; the belt floor draws one side only, so the walls in front and the top were left out. Now each side is turned to face away from the box's middle, whatever the placing does (`box-geometry.spec.ts`, test first). A fix of its own: commit it apart (`fix(web)`).

## Also fixed: a belt starting where no building stands

Seen 2026-11-19 on run `01a1f30d`: a red dot for Caterium ore inside the area, where there's no miner; the miner (`…2147320151`, at 2251, −1287) stands outside. `SupplyBelts.Merge` put the miner on one belt with another source of Caterium ore, however far apart (the first, then the nearest that still fit); the merged spot was the rectangle around both, it touched the area, so no existing belt was followed, and the router started the belt anywhere in it. Now only buildings within **50 metres** of the first on a belt (along x and y) share it (`TogetherCentimetres`; test first, two tests in `SupplyBeltsTests`); one further away keeps a belt of its own, so its dot stands at it, and one outside comes in along its own existing belt. With supply lines, their ore still runs together on the floor, at a priority merger. BR-O2 says so. 50 metres is my choice: neighbouring nodes stay together; overrule it if you like. **A new run is needed to see it.** A fix of its own (`fix(world)`).

## Also fixed: existing belts drawn as zigzags

Seen 2026-11-19 in 3D on run `01a1f411`: the existing belt from a miner outside the area ran in zigzags (most on Miner Mk.3 `…2146973172`, with stretches of 16–33 m between the belt's points). `ExistingWays.PointsOf` joined a belt's spline points with straight lines; in the game the belt curves between them, along each point's leave and arrive tangents. ## Also fixed: a bottleneck offered 222,152/min on top

Seen 2026-11-19 in the findings of snapshot `01a1f07a`: Conveyor Lift Mk.6 `…2146922344`, carrying aluminum casings from four train stations, at 1200 of 1200 with **222,152/min offered on top**. The cause was in the simulation, not the finding: a splitter offers each output nearly all it's offered (less what the other outputs take for sure), and a merger adds up what its inputs offer. So a line split and merged again counted the same offer twice, every balancer stage doubled it, and no belt brought it back down. Now a belt, lift or pipe offers on no more than it can carry (`Solver.AtMost`, BR-A12), test first: `A_belt_offers_on_no_more_than_it_can_carry_so_a_split_and_merged_line_is_not_counted_twice`. The analysis version goes to 14, so every stored analysis is computed again.

Replayed on that snapshot: the lift now has **984/min** on top, the largest anywhere is 1200, and there are 55 bottlenecks instead of 93. **8 oil refineries** (pure iron ingot, `…2146972300`–`303` and `…2146648243`–`246`) go from 100% to **15%, output blocked**, in the model. That's the same bug showing elsewhere: the 12 converters they feed are starved of SAM ingots (6.7–60%, unchanged), yet each took 100 iron ingots a minute, 1200 in all, while using 180. The inflated SAM offer made them accept iron as if running at 100%, and 1020 ingots a minute vanished. Now they take what they use, and the refineries' iron has nowhere else to go.

*Known limit:* a split and merged line can still be offered up to what its belts carry, more than the source has; the cap only stops the multiplying. Exact would need to know where each offer comes from. A fix of its own (`fix(world)`).

**Then a bottleneck must hold something back** (you decided 2026-11-19: "I would not call that a bottleneck"). Conveyor Lift Mk.6 `…2147251914` ends a plastic manifold of 28 oil refineries making 2231/min, all at 100% in the model: the lift is full, but what it can't take goes the other way through smart splitters' overflow. Now a full belt, lift or pipe is listed only when, upstream of it through belts, splitters and mergers, a miner or extractor delivers less than 99.9% of its offer, or a machine is output blocked (`Solver.HoldsBack`, test first: `A_full_belt_is_no_bottleneck_when_what_it_cant_take_goes_another_way`). The 99.9% is the same noise rule as a machine's (BR-A11): a copper miner filling ten Mk.2 belts exactly settled at 1199.99988 of 1200, and each belt showed up with 0.0001 on top. `Belts_that_a_miner_fills_exactly_are_no_bottleneck` passed straight away (the small network settles exactly); the replay is the evidence. A train station never counts, since its rate isn't known. Replayed on `01a1f07a`: **93 bottlenecks before, 8 now**, each a Mk.1 lift or Mk.3 belt holding back a miner. Machines are unchanged by this part. Analysis version 15 (the app had already run with 14 in between), same `fix(world)` commit.

## Choices made (overrule if you like)

- Miner red `#e53935`. The fluid's `mFluidColor`, not its `mGasColor`: for a gas such as Nitrogen the gas colour is white. Alpha is ignored.
- Every miner the same red, not a colour per ore: the game gives ores no colour, and the belt's colour already tells which factory it feeds.
